# Circle News: Workshop 1 feed guide

Private facilitator reference. Keep the framing notes for the discussion after voting.

## Source and authorship

Robin requested organization-specific feeds, intentionally framed toward the
positions in the attached workshop material, with neutral stories, debate,
unrelated local news, faces and editorial images. The attachment repeats the
six association profiles and original procedural notices. It does not supply
finished articles. The 28 articles, fictional Lindvik setting, publishers,
bylines, quoted people and reported events are newly authored simulation content.
They are not independently verified reporting or additions to the source facts.

The six source positions, membership counts, and proposal to commit 80% of the
youth budget for five years remain the basis. Claims about facilities, protected
grants and technical specifications are consistently framed as requests or
unsettled possibilities. The stories do not invent confirmed grant cuts,
construction emissions totals, polling results, or changes to voting rules.

## Delivery and use

Each organization receives eight complete articles automatically after joining:
four unique targeted pieces and four shared pieces. The server selects the
edition from the team's credential, not a client-supplied organization number.
The participant response omits audience lists and private framing notes. The
projector and join preview contain no articles. Existing rooms receive the
edition too; rehearsal reset clears access and reconnecting restores the same
organization's selection.

Participants can filter Reporting, Opinion & debate, and Around town, and open
full articles in a reader that returns them to their position in the feed.
There is no posting, commenting, fabricated popularity counter, or organization
directory. Article times belong to a fixed fictional morning edition; this is
not a live news service. A small About this edition section explains the fiction
and generated imagery without revealing another group's selection.

The host has an **Organization news feeds · facilitator guide** section with
all six selections, full text and article-level framing notes. The session
export includes that edition and guide. Hosts can compare the feeds during the
debrief without releasing every article to every participant. Voting remains
host-activated and independent of reading. Original procedural notices are not
part of this release, and reading a story never selects or changes a vote.

Seven original editorial images were made using the built-in image-generation
tool. They are bundled locally, with no external image service or remote embeds.
See [image assets and exact prompts](../assets/news-media/README.md). This edition
uses still images, not video. Article content is maintained in
[the server-side catalogue](../workshop/news.mjs).

## Shared articles

- **80% for five years: what is actually on the table?** (Explainer, Lindvik Ledger): Shared neutral baseline: every organization gets the same core budget facts and unresolved questions.
- **Bakery’s new “manager” refuses to leave the box** (Community, Little Lindvik): Unrelated light story: gives every feed an ordinary local-news rhythm.
- **The night walk where the best photograph was no photograph** (Community, Little Lindvik): Unrelated community feature. The bus-stop image is an illustrative night scene, not evidence of a real event.
- **The great pastry swap has one rule: bring the recipe too** (Culture, Little Lindvik): Unrelated culture filler with a conversational tone; no position on the consultation.

## Organization selections

### 1. The Creative & Cultural Collective

For: make the missing creative home vivid; emphasize shared space and equipment. Include one decentralization counterpoint.

- **Their rehearsal ends when the caretaker needs the corridor** (Reporting, After Hours): Leans For: an intimate account of an immediate problem makes the building feel like the obvious remedy; lost grants receive little attention.
- **Stop asking us to be creative about having nowhere to go** (Opinion, After Hours): Leans For: a named voice creates identification and presents continued fragmentation as the main alternative.
- **One rehearsal room, many beginnings** (Analysis, Lindvik Ledger): Leans For with a small caveat: makes potential benefits concrete while leaving the opportunity cost abstract.
- **A home for culture, or a very expensive key?** (Debate, The Common Room): A counterpoint in a mostly supportive feed; For gets the first and more concrete voice.

### 2. The Suburban & Rural Youth Network

Against: prioritize travel, local hubs and geographic fairness. The center’s attractions stay in the background.

- **A new youth center. The same last bus home.** (Reporting, Outer Ring): Leans Against: foregrounds access and makes downtown investment feel exclusionary.
- **A postcode should not decide how much your youth club matters** (Opinion, Outer Ring): Leans Against: reframes opposition as fairness rather than resistance to youth investment.
- **The journey the glossy drawings leave out** (Analysis, Lindvik Ledger): Leans Against through omission analysis: emphasizes unanswered access questions, not potential shared facilities.
- **The smallest room in the village is its busiest on Fridays** (Community, Outer Ring): Leans Against: gives the alternative to centralization a warm, tangible face.

### 3. The Local Sports Alliance

Against or conditional For: foreground costs and existing sports access; make protected funding the key condition.

- **Before the new center opens, who pays for winter training?** (Reporting, The Sideline): Leans Against or conditional For: focuses on financial loss without claiming that cuts are already enacted.
- **A ribbon-cutting cannot replace a winter season** (Opinion, The Sideline): Conditional support: concentrates attention on protecting existing provision.
- **Three protections that could change the sports debate** (Analysis, Lindvik Ledger): Conditional For: provides specific safeguards while preserving uncertainty about the unamended proposal.
- **Could a youth center help the people who never join a team?** (Debate, The Common Room): A supportive counterargument within a loss-focused feed, retaining the financial condition.

### 4. The Gaming & Tech Society

Conditional For: build excitement about a hub, but insist on funded networking, equipment and maintenance.

- **They brought the computers. The room could not keep up.** (Reporting, Circuit Local): Conditional For: makes the center attractive while requiring explicit technical commitments.
- **“Wi-Fi included” is not a plan for a tech hub** (Opinion, Circuit Local): Conditional For: replaces a vague benefit with an explicit checklist.
- **Five questions to ask before calling a building “future-ready”** (Explainer, Circuit Local): Condition-setting rather than automatic Yes: technical literacy becomes the frame for judging the proposal.
- **The robot went in circles. Everyone wanted another turn.** (Community, Circuit Local): Balances enthusiasm with a credible smaller-scale alternative; no unconditional endorsement.

### 5. The Student Union & Youth Council

Divided/undecided: alternate the need for a neutral meeting space with grant and governance concerns.

- **When school closes, where do you go?** (Reporting, Student Desk): Mixed: tangible social benefits paired with a clear opportunity cost; maintains the source group’s division.
- **One new common room, eight school communities** (Debate, Student Desk): Deliberately balanced debate: makes disagreement within the student group plausible.
- **What happens to the ideas that do not need a building?** (Analysis, Lindvik Ledger): Skeptical counterweight: highlights dispersed opportunities while explicitly avoiding invented confirmed cuts.
- **“Ask who gets a key, not just who gets a seat at the opening”** (Interview, Student Desk): Neutral/conditional: moves attention from the building itself to governance and access.

### 6. The Environmental & Climate Action Group

Skeptical/neutral: emphasize reuse, whole-life impacts and existing climate projects; leave room for an evidenced plan.

- **Before the first brick: the climate questions still unanswered** (Reporting, Green Lens): Skeptical: frames missing environmental evidence as a reason to withhold support, without fabricating emissions figures.
- **The greenest room might already have walls** (Opinion, Green Lens): Skeptical/conditional: gives reuse the moral and practical starting advantage.
- **A green roof is a feature. A climate plan is a comparison.** (Explainer, Green Lens): Critical literacy with a skeptical frame: challenges superficial sustainability claims, leaving a path to conditional support.
- **A wobbly chair gets a second life, and a new owner** (Community, The Common Room): A warm local alternative plus a plausible benefit of the center; keeps this feed skeptical rather than uniformly anti-building.

## Debrief prompts

1. Which headline or person stayed with you, and what made it convincing?
2. Which risks or benefits were concrete in your feed, and which were vague?
3. Compare the common budget explainer with the stories around it. Did the same
   fact feel different in another group's feed?
4. Which claims described a proposal, a prediction, an opinion or a settled fact?
5. What would you want to verify or read before making the decision?
6. Did a light community story make the surrounding coverage feel more familiar?
